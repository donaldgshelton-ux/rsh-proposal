01 · The Premise

You already know where it hurts.
The question is why.

R-S-H runs complex, multi-discipline projects across gas transmission, specialty chemical, and pulp and paper. The technical work is excellent. The friction is everywhere else — in the coordination overhead, the after-hours check-ins, the meetings that exist to surface information that should have been surfaced before the meeting, and the institutional knowledge that lives almost entirely in the president's head because there's nowhere else to put it.

None of that is a people problem. It is a systems problem. And systems problems have maps.

This engagement begins with a diagnostic. The diagnostic produces the map. What R-S-H does with that map — which paths to take, how far to go, whether to bring this consultant along for the journey — is entirely R-S-H's call. That is how it should be.

02 · Phase One

The Diagnostic

A structured engagement — conversations with key principals, review of two or three active projects, observation of the current workflow. The output is a written assessment that does three things:

  • Names the specific friction points consuming the most time and mental bandwidth — not generically, but in the language of R-S-H's actual projects and client relationships
  • Prioritizes them by impact — which ones, if addressed, would most change what the president's week looks like
  • Recommends specific tools and methods for each — drawn from the menu described in the next section, or developed specifically for what the diagnostic reveals

Some friction points will be invisible until someone looks from the outside. Others are already known — the daily urgencies just don't leave room to address them. Getting the job done is always the first priority; the structural improvements get deferred because there is no capacity to pursue them while the fires are burning. The diagnostic creates the capacity to finally see both at once.

03 · The Tool Menu

What the Map
May Recommend

The diagnostic will determine which of these apply and in what priority. This is not a package — it is a menu. R-S-H selects what fits. Each item addresses a specific, named friction point.

RAG Knowledge Base

Institutional memory made queryable. Transcripts, contracts, specs, correspondence — searchable in plain language. "Who's handling the instrumentation spec on the Sterlington job?" becomes a search, not a phone call.

Meeting Intelligence Pipeline

Every recorded coordination call produces a structured summary within one hour: decisions made, action items by party, open questions, blockers. No more need for someone to manually write and distribute meeting notes — the AI output, trained specifically on R-S-H's operations and terminology, is reviewed, edited if needed, and sent. Done.

Pre-Meeting Prep Packages

Before each scheduled call, a structured agenda drawn from open items. Questions pre-assigned by party. The client PM arrives knowing what is expected. Meeting duration drops because discovery happens before the room, not in it.

Pre-Call Intelligence Brief

Before a PM call, a summary of that project's full history, open items, and a suggested question framework — so the president walks in oriented, not starting from memory.

Scope Capture Pipeline

After a scope-extraction conversation, the transcript becomes a structured draft — ready for review, correction, and delivery. The translation work still happens in the call. Nothing gets lost after it.

Stakeholder Accountability System

Between meetings: who owes what, documented and tracked. Vendors with outstanding deliverables get structured follow-up. Every request timestamped. The paper trail that protects R-S-H contractually — and stops the "I'll have that next week" drift.

Project Status Digest

A single AI-assembled weekly view across active projects — what's moving, what's blocked, who's behind. Not new software. An intelligent layer over the data that already exists.

Scope Creep Early Warning

Flags when meeting transcripts show expanding expectations without corresponding change orders — before the cost lands on R-S-H's side of the ledger.

Pattern Analysis & Trend Intelligence

Across months of meeting and project data: which vendors consistently delay, which project types always stall at the same stage, which client relationships generate disproportionate coordination overhead, and which internal processes are quietly working well and worth replicating. What the data says over time that no individual meeting ever reveals.

Custom Tools

The diagnostic may reveal friction points not on this list. New tools can be designed and built specifically for what R-S-H needs. This menu is a starting point, not a ceiling — and the map recommends, R-S-H selects what fits.

04 · Phase Two

06 · To Begin

A Few Questions
Before the Diagnostic

The natural starting point is a single working conversation — 60 to 90 minutes — to walk two or three active projects and identify where the friction is highest. From that conversation, the diagnostic scope is confirmed and the engagement begins.

A few things that will shape the work:

  • Which projects or client relationships generate the most recurring coordination overhead — and do those clients typically have dedicated project managers, or does that role vary by engagement?
  • Is there an existing project management platform, or is the workflow primarily Teams and email?
  • Are there pain points beyond the meeting and coordination layer — other places where time or information is consistently getting lost?

The map will show R-S-H what is there. Where to go on that map — and whether to bring this consultant along for the journey — is entirely your decision. Based on what I already know about this firm and the problems it is solving, it should be an interesting one.
